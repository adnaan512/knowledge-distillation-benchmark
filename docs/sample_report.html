Key Findings

RQ1 — Feature vs Response: Feature-based distillation shows advantages on fine-grained variants where intermediate spatial features carry discriminative information. Response-based is more robust across all compression levels.
Counter-intuitive result: On the full student, response-based distillation outperformed relation-based by 1.1%. Despite relation-based distillation encoding richer structural information, the student at this compression level lacks the capacity to faithfully reproduce the teacher's pairwise geometry. Simpler, direct probability supervision (soft labels) provides a more learnable training signal when the student is severely capacity-constrained. This is a capacity bottleneck problem, not a knowledge-richness problem.

Research Questions — Summary Answers

RQQuestionAnswer
RQ1 Does feature-based distillation outperform response-based on fine-grained tasks? Mixed — feature-based wins at moderate compression; response-based is more robust at high compression.
RQ2 What is the optimal temperature T for response-based distillation? T ∈ [4, 8] consistently outperforms T=1 and T=16. Exact optimum shifts slightly with compression ratio.
RQ3 Which compression ratio represents the best accuracy-efficiency knee point? The "half" variant (~1.4M params, ~17× compression) offers the best trade-off. "Quarter" crosses into accuracy-loss territory.
